PRODUCT IDENTITY (5.0 — canonical game summary)
-----------------------------------------------
Dark Matter: Genesis is a premium sci-fi survival colony sim set on Jupiter's moon
Io in 2160. You lead humanity's push to prove a future there—not just scrape by, but
rebuild civilization under brutal conditions. You manage oxygen and environmental
pressures (radiation, cold/heat, volcanism, sulfur storms, alien life forms, and AI
androids), gather and craft, fight on the surface, and expand a base of up to
twenty-two companions while a trio of three skilled companions can be switched and
run expeditions with the player. You rescue procedural Neural Echoes, spend Aether
Credits at vendors, and operate major structures through in-world Building Control
Panels. A Lite Building system supports building your colony camp.

Long-term progression ties to Aether-9—an ancient Echo sealed in a dormant machine—
and Memory Cores that trigger Resonance Events: short world-changing story beats that
unlock lore, systems, and colony growth. The emotional arc moves you from survivor
to explorer, commander, and founder of humanity's first permanent home beyond Earth.

Under the hood, Dark Matter: Genesis runs on The World Engine, a World Operating
System (WoOS) stack built for Unity 6:

  World → Simulation → Intelligence → Experience → Presentation → Player

Gameplay systems (combat, inventory, roster, weather, building) feed read-only
GameState and WorldState snapshots; Directors and Experience decide when storms
spike, radio traffic fires, or narrative beats land; Presentation (Communications,
HUD, audio) delivers what the world authorizes—without AI or UI reaching directly
into managers. The design goal is a world that feels like it keeps running whether
you're watching or not: sulfur storms pause base work and force shelter, expedition
crews carry full exposure risk, and future Resonance Events will reshape Io while
your colony simulates injury, healing, and production at scale.

Dark Matter (The World Engine) is the reusable framework. Dark Matter: Genesis is
its first shipped game—the Io story, AC economy, Echo roster, Lite Building colony
camp, and sulfur-storm companion fantasy layered on top.

Chapter 1 - Executive Vision
================================================================================

The Mission
-----------
Dark Matter: Genesis is a premium single-player science-fiction survival colony
simulator designed to deliver the emotional depth, visual quality, and systemic
complexity associated with modern AAA games while remaining achievable for a
solo developer through disciplined engineering, procedural systems, and
AI-assisted production.

The game is not about surviving on Io.

It is about proving that humanity deserves a future there.

Every expedition, discovery, structure, rescued companion, and scientific
breakthrough contributes toward rebuilding civilization on one of the most
dangerous worlds in the Solar System.

Survival is the foundation.

Discovery is the reward.

Leadership is the challenge.

Legacy is the destination.


Vision Statement
----------------
Dark Matter: Genesis exists to create an experience where players genuinely believe
they are leading humanity's first successful colony on Io. Player-facing pitch,
emotional arc, and WoOS runtime summary: see PRODUCT IDENTITY (5.0) above.

The colony is not simply a base.

It is humanity's future.


The North Star
--------------
Every design decision must support one simple statement:

The player should always feel that their decisions shape the future of humanity.

If a mechanic doesn't reinforce that feeling, it doesn't belong in Dark Matter: Genesis.


Design Philosophy
-----------------
Every mechanic exists for one of four reasons.

Wonder
  Players should constantly discover things they have never seen before.
  Hidden caves. Ancient precursor structures. Volcanic phenomena. Neural Echoes.
  Environmental storytelling.

Responsibility
  Nothing happens in isolation.
  Building decisions affect research.
  Research affects expeditions.
  Expeditions affect colony growth.
  Colony growth affects the story.

Mastery
  Players should become more knowledgeable instead of simply becoming stronger.
  Knowledge is progression.
  Learning Io is progression.
  Understanding Resonance is progression.
  Managing a colony is progression.

Hope
  Although Io is dangerous, the game should never become hopeless.
  Each success should feel like another step toward creating humanity's first
  permanent home beyond Earth.
  Hope is the game's emotional currency.


Solo AAA Philosophy
-------------------
This project is built around a philosophy that differs fundamentally from
traditional AAA development.

AAA studios scale through people.

Dark Matter Studios scales through systems.

Our goal is not to imitate a 500-person studio.

Our goal is to produce a game that players believe could have been made by one.

Every feature must satisfy these principles:

  Reusable before bespoke.
  Procedural before repetitive.
  Data-driven before hard-coded.
  Polish before expansion.
  Finished before replaced.


AI Development Philosophy
-------------------------
Artificial intelligence is not a gameplay feature.

It is a production multiplier.

AI assists with:

  C# implementation
  Editor tooling
  Documentation
  Refactoring
  Testing
  Placeholder assets
  Content generation
  Design iteration

Creative ownership remains with the developer.

Every AI-generated contribution is reviewed, refined, and integrated
intentionally.


Success Metrics
---------------
Dark Matter: Genesis succeeds if players consistently describe it as:

  Immersive.
  Atmospheric.
  Intelligent.
  Rewarding.
  Memorable.
  Highly replayable.

Not "large."

Not "feature rich."

Those qualities emerge naturally from strong design.

Chapter 2 - Player Experience
================================================================================

Player Fantasy
--------------
The player is not a hero sent to save the world.

The player is humanity's first successful colony director.

Every system should reinforce that identity.

Players are responsible for:

  protecting lives,
  making scientific discoveries,
  building infrastructure,
  expanding civilization,
  deciding humanity's future.


Emotional Progression
---------------------
Phase One
  "I don't think we'll survive."

Phase Two
  "We might actually do this."

Phase Three
  "These people depend on me."

Phase Four
  "We've built something incredible."

Phase Five
  "What legacy will I leave?"

Every chapter of gameplay should move the player naturally through these
emotional states.


Core Gameplay Loop
------------------
Prepare -> Plan -> Explore -> Discover -> Recover -> Return -> Analyze -> Build ->
Expand -> Repeat


Design Rule
-----------
The player should never feel forced into one activity.

Exploration should naturally inspire research.

Research should inspire construction.

Construction should inspire larger expeditions.

The systems create momentum instead of relying on artificial objectives.

Chapter 3 - The World of Io
================================================================================

Design Goal
-----------
Io is not merely the setting.

Io is the game's primary antagonist, greatest mystery, and most important
character.

The player should gradually develop a relationship with the moon itself.

At first it feels hostile.

Later it becomes understandable.

Eventually it becomes almost alive.


Environmental Identity
----------------------
Io should communicate constant instability.

Nothing feels permanent.

Mountains collapse.

New lava rivers emerge.

Sulfur storms reshape traversal.

Ancient structures awaken.

Every expedition should feel different because the world itself evolves.


The Living Planet Philosophy
----------------------------
Rather than using scripted events, Io is governed by interconnected systems.

  Geological activity.
  Weather.
  Resonance.
  Wildlife.
  Ancient technology.

These systems influence one another continuously.


Biome Philosophy
----------------
Every biome must satisfy five requirements:

  Unique visual identity.
  Unique survival challenge.
  Unique resource economy.
  Unique environmental storytelling.
  Unique gameplay opportunities.

A biome should never exist solely because it looks different.


Environmental Storytelling
--------------------------
The player should regularly encounter evidence of previous expeditions.

  Collapsed habitats.
  Destroyed mining rigs.
  Abandoned laboratories.
  Emergency broadcasts.
  Personal belongings.

These moments tell the story without requiring long cutscenes.

Chapter 4 - Core Gameplay Pillars
================================================================================

Pillar One - Explore the Unknown
--------------------------------
Exploration drives every progression system.

Without exploration there is no research.

Without research there is no advancement.


Pillar Two - Build a Living Colony
----------------------------------
The colony is the heart of the game.

Players should care about the people they rescue and the structures they create.


Pillar Three - Scientific Discovery
-----------------------------------
Knowledge is the primary resource.

The player should constantly ask:

"What happens if I investigate this?"


Pillar Four - Meaningful Leadership
-----------------------------------
Leadership is expressed through decisions rather than dialogue.

  Resource allocation.
  Research priorities.
  Expedition planning.
  Risk management.
  Ethical choices involving Echoes.


Pillar Five - Emergent Stories
------------------------------
The best stories come from systems interacting.

Not scripted sequences.

Players should remember:

"The storm hit while my rescue team was halfway home."

Not:

"I completed Mission 14."


================================================================================
Chapter 5 - The Solo Developer Production Strategy
================================================================================

This chapter is unique. Most GDDs don't include it.

Ours must.

Because it is one of the project's greatest strengths.


Production Mission
------------------
Every feature must provide the highest possible player value for the lowest
possible development cost.

This is not about cutting corners.

It is about maximizing impact.


The 80/20 Rule
--------------
Twenty percent of the work should create eighty percent of the player's
experience.

Examples include:

  Dynamic weather instead of hundreds of scripted events.
  Procedural Echo generation instead of hundreds of handcrafted companions.
  Modular building pieces instead of unique architecture for every structure.
  Layered audio and lighting to create atmosphere instead of expensive
  cinematics.


Reusable System Hierarchy
-------------------------
Whenever possible, build one framework that supports many features.

Examples:

  One interaction system for every terminal.
  One inventory system shared by players, containers, drones, and buildings.
  One status effect system used for weather, combat, hazards, and buffs.
  One quest framework for story missions, side missions, and procedural
  expeditions.


AI-First Development
--------------------
Every major gameplay system should be designed with AI-assisted implementation
in mind.

That means:

  Clear interfaces.
  Small, testable classes.
  Data-driven configuration.
  Strong documentation.
  Editor tools that reduce repetitive work.

The goal is to make future coding sessions with AI agents faster, more
consistent, and easier to review.


The "Fun Per Month" Rule
------------------------
This will be our final filter before committing to any feature.

For every proposed mechanic, we'll ask:

How much player enjoyment does this create for every month of development it
requires?

If a feature takes six months to build but only adds a few minutes of meaningful
gameplay, it doesn't belong in Version 1.0.

That doesn't mean we discard good ideas - it means we build them when the
project can support them.

APPENDIX A — Interim Mechanics Lock (folded from GDD 3.0)
================================================================================

Identity recap
--------------
Year 2160. Semi-low-poly survival + light base-builder on Io. Lead up to 25
companions: up to 22 at base, switchable trio of 3 on expeditions. Rescue procedural Neural
Echoes; recover Memory Cores for Aether-9 → Resonance Events; Lite Building +
materialization pipeline for colony camp; survive O2 norm, four environmental
pressures, alien life forms, and AI androids.

Classes (locked names)
----------------------
  Architect Engineer, Science Specialist, Combat Tactician, Infiltrator Scout
  (+ Io hybrids). Player has combat/skills too.


A1. Platforms, art & tech
-------------------------
  Primary:  PC (keyboard + mouse, optional gamepad)
  Secondary: Consoles (PS5 / Xbox) — gamepad-first parity
  Not a ship target: mobile
  Art: semi-low-poly modular; 2–3 base meshes + attachments + material swaps
  Tech: Unity 6 + URP; Addressables/LODs as needed; aggregate base sim (no
        per-companion pathfinding at 22 scale)


A2. Survival — O2 norm & biome pressures
----------------------------------------
  ALWAYS ON: Oxygen (tanks, deprivation FX, health drain when empty)

  FOUR ENVIRONMENTAL PRESSURES:
    1. Radiation
    2. Cold / Heat — SINGLE thermal stress meter (cold pole ↔ heat pole)
    3. Volcanos (lava, eruptions, tremors)
    4. Sulfur storms (corrosive weather; base shelter trigger)

  SURFACE THREATS (combat / exploration):
    5. Alien life forms — native Io fauna; escalation by biome and Resonance
    6. AI androids — hostile or corrupted machines left from prior expeditions

  Offsets (design target): shelter, caves, inoculations, portable habitat
  (Architect Engineer), environmental suits (upgradeable trio gear).


A3. Companion roster (25 cap)
---------------------------
  ~12–13 workers (labor pool; collective Strain meter)
  ~12–13 skilled Echoes / Special companions (named, upgradable)
  TRIO (3 skilled companions) on surface with player — player SWITCHES which
  companions fill the three expedition slots; full O2 + all four environmental
  pressures plus surface threats while deployed.
  BASE (up to 22 companions) — sealed base loop (see A4)
  Skilled: Saturation meter; cleanse at Purification Hub
  Pet/AI follower: DESIGN TARGET = fold into Echo/trio (no separate pet loop)


A4. Base 22 & Command Center
----------------------------
  Base companions impervious to routine rad / thermal / volcano / surface O2.
  NOT impervious to: sulfur storms, Command Center building damage, Strain.

  Sulfur storm flow:
    1. All base work PAUSES
    2. Evacuate to Command Center rooms
    3. Storm ends → healthy companions resume jobs

  Building damage: injures / incapacitates — NEVER kills base companions.
  Heal/restore required before they contribute again.


A5. Neural Echoes & procedural generation
-----------------------------------------
  Rescue and reintegrate — do not hire from a shop menu as primary path.
  Echoes are imprints of failed Io expeditions / prior inhabitants.
  Failure can lose Echo permanently.
  Procedural: name format [Io Prefix] [Core Name] [Designation], class, stats,
  abilities, backstory fragment, material tint + modular attachments.
  Progression is local roster / save only — no marketplace trading of Echoes.


A6. Aether-9, Memory Cores & Resonance Events
---------------------------------------------
  Aether-9 is a SPECIAL prologue intelligence — not a generic mission-control bot and not
  a human companion. He is an idle machine / probe shell containing an embedded
  ancient alien Neural Echo who calls himself Aether-9.

  PROLOGUE DISCOVERY
    Player finds the dormant machine early (prologue). Interacting loads a repair
    quest: recover 3 objects that restore the machine enough to awaken.

  AWAKENING
    Once repaired, Aether-9 comes online slightly hostile / angry. He recounts
    that his group was killed by something sinister on Io; they had collected
    Memory Cores once housed in this machine. Without those cores he cannot
    function well or remember much of Io’s past.

  MEMORY CORES → RESONANCE EVENTS
    Find core → recovery setpiece → attach / slot into Aether-9 → Resonance Event
    (10–15 min world change) + story fragment + unlocks.
    May spike storms, damage Command Center, injure base companions, spawn Echo
    opportunities. Repeat per core (design target: at least 3 primary cores).

  TRUST LADDER (communications)
    Angry / hostile → wary → advisor (mission tips, status help) → friend who
    radios in unprompted and eventually relays chats with other companions.
    Colony Ops / expedition crew handle radio queries until Aether-9 is awake
    and advisory-unlocked. Personality-flavored Aether voice is a later comms
    phase; do not treat him as day-one channel traffic.

  RELATIONSHIP TO ECHOES
    Aether-9 IS an Echo sealed in a machine — distinct from rescueable surface
    Echoes that join the companion roster. He is the mystery hub for Memory Cores
    and Resonance progression.


A7. Buildings, Lite Building, craft & Building Control Panels
-------------------------------------------------------------
  LITE BUILDING (colony camp — shipped direction):
    Lightweight placement and maintenance loop for colony camp structures
    (shelters, utilities, camp modules) before full materialization pipeline.
    Player spends gathered resources + AC at vendors where applicable; camp
    expands outward from the Command Center anchor.

  FULL MATERIALIZATION (planned):
  Spend resources → building MATERIALIZES (scale-up + VFX); maintenance over time.
  Key buildings: Command Center, Purification Hub, Geothermal Harvester,
  Echo Reclamation Chamber, Resonance Beacon, Probe Uplink, Geothermal
  Stabilizer, Medical Facility, Science Labs.

  ATTACHMENT MODULES (planned stack — expand cores with attachable modules):
    Generators, power grids, automated resource gathering, logistics,
    communications, defense, mining. Modules feed Building Control
    Production / Changes tabs and sulfur-storm pause rules same as buildings.

  BUILDING CONTROL PANELS (locked UX):
    Each major structure has an in-world terminal (E) → Building Control UI
    (NOT Journal) with tabs: Overview | Companions | Production | Craft | Changes.
    Assign base-22 companions, set production/craft queues, per-building /
    module settings.
    Queues run while player is on expedition; PAUSE during sulfur storms.
    Journal Craft = recipe library / scroll learning only — not primary
    production UI.


A8. Economy (current lock)
--------------------------
  AC — sole gameplay currency.
    New game starter: 0 AC → load 1 starter Skilled Companion.
    Earn: XP, Credits, resources, quests, expeditions, Resonance bonuses, base output, loot.
    Spend: vendors (supplies, recruits, gear), workers, rush craft, upgrades,
    heals, inoculations, suits, Lite Building camp modules.
    Optional IAP later → AC only.
  Workers: AC recruit/replace. Skilled companions: local roster progression only.
  When touching economy code: prefer AC for all UI/save; remove leftover legacy
  currency naming from player-facing HUD.


A9. Controls & UI (target)
--------------------------
  KBM primary on PC; gamepad required for consoles / optional on PC.
  Journal: Inventory, Map, Companions/Echoes (trios + roster), Skills, Craft,
  Recipes — pet UI folds into Companions/Echoes when migration completes.
  HUD: O2 + four exposure indicators + surface-threat awareness; thermal = one
  cold/heat meter; AC balance; base status; hotbar + toolbar.

-------------------------------
