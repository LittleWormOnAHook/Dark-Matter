PRODUCT IDENTITY (5.0 — canonical game summary)
-----------------------------------------------
Dark Matter: Genesis is a companion-driven survival RPG — a story-driven sci-fi RPG
with a base camp on Jupiter's moon Io in 2160. You play as **Kade**, leading humanity's push to prove a
future there—not just scrape by, but rebuild under brutal conditions. You manage
oxygen and environmental pressures (radiation, cold/heat, volcanism) plus dynamic Io
weather (sulfur storms, ion lightning, ash gales, dust spouts, lava flows, geyser
surges, eruptions, tremors), alien life forms, and AI androids, gather and craft,
fight on the surface, and grow a base camp of up to twenty-two companions while a
trio of three skilled companions can be switched and run expeditions with the player.
You rescue procedural Neural Echoes, spend Aether Credits at vendors, and operate
major structures through in-world Building Control Panels. A Lite Building system
supports expanding your base camp.

Long-term progression ties to **Kairos**—an ancient Echo sealed in a dormant machine—
and Memory Cores that trigger Resonance Events: short world-changing story beats that
unlock lore, systems, and base camp growth. Pre-prologue UI labels the dormant shell as
liaison machine / probe / ledger contact; **Kade learns the name Kairos at prologue end**.
The emotional arc moves Kade from survivor
to explorer, leader, and founder of humanity's first permanent home beyond Earth.

Under the hood, Dark Matter: Genesis runs on The World Engine, a World Operating
System (WoOS) stack built for Unity 6 + HDRP:

  World → Simulation → Intelligence → Experience → Presentation → Player

Gameplay systems (combat, inventory, roster, weather, building) feed read-only
GameState and WorldState snapshots; Directors and Experience decide when storms
spike, radio traffic fires, or narrative beats land; Presentation (Communications,
HUD, audio) delivers what the world authorizes—without AI or UI reaching directly
into managers. The design goal is a world that feels like it keeps running whether
you're watching or not: sulfur storms and Resonance Supercells pause base work and
force shelter; ash gales, lightning, lava surges, and geyser fields threaten
expeditions; crews carry full exposure risk, and future Resonance Events will
reshape Io while your base camp simulates injury, healing, and production at scale.

Dark Matter (The World Engine) is the reusable framework. Dark Matter: Genesis is
its first shipped game—the Io story, AC economy, Echo roster, Lite Building base
camp, and sulfur-storm companion fantasy layered on top.

Chapter 1 - Executive Vision
================================================================================

The Mission
-----------
Dark Matter: Genesis is a premium single-player companion-driven survival RPG —
a story-driven sci-fi RPG with a base camp — designed to deliver the emotional
depth, visual quality, and systemic complexity associated with modern AAA games
while remaining achievable for a solo developer through disciplined engineering,
procedural systems, and AI-assisted production.

The game is not about surviving on Io.

It is about proving that humanity deserves a future there.

Every expedition, discovery, structure, rescued companion, and scientific
breakthrough contributes toward rebuilding civilization on one of the most
dangerous worlds in the Solar System.

Survival is the foundation.

Discovery is the reward.

Leadership is the challenge.

Legacy is the destination.


Vision Statement
----------------
Dark Matter: Genesis exists to create an experience where players genuinely believe
they are founding humanity's first foothold on Io — **Kade**, commander of a base camp and
the companions who depend on them. Player-facing pitch, emotional arc, and WoOS
runtime summary: see PRODUCT IDENTITY (5.0) above.

The base camp is not simply a build menu.

It is humanity's future.


The North Star
--------------
Every design decision must support one simple statement:

The player should always feel that their decisions shape the future of humanity.

If a mechanic doesn't reinforce that feeling, it doesn't belong in Dark Matter: Genesis.


Design Philosophy
-----------------
Every mechanic exists for one of four reasons.

Wonder
  Players should constantly discover things they have never seen before.
  Hidden caves. Ancient precursor structures. Volcanic phenomena. Neural Echoes.
  Environmental storytelling.

Responsibility
  Nothing happens in isolation.
  Building decisions affect research.
  Research affects expeditions.
  Expeditions affect base camp growth.
  Base camp growth affects the story.

Mastery
  Players should become more knowledgeable instead of simply becoming stronger.
  Knowledge is progression.
  Learning Io is progression.
  Understanding Resonance is progression.
  Growing and protecting your base camp is progression.

Hope
  Although Io is dangerous, the game should never become hopeless.
  Each success should feel like another step toward creating humanity's first
  permanent home beyond Earth.
  Hope is the game's emotional currency.


Solo AAA Philosophy
-------------------
This project is built around a philosophy that differs fundamentally from
traditional AAA development.

AAA studios scale through people.

Dark Matter Studios scales through systems.

Our goal is not to imitate a 500-person studio.

Our goal is to produce a game that players believe could have been made by one.

Every feature must satisfy these principles:

  Reusable before bespoke.
  Procedural before repetitive.
  Data-driven before hard-coded.
  Polish before expansion.
  Finished before replaced.


AI Development Philosophy
-------------------------
Artificial intelligence is not a gameplay feature.

It is a production multiplier.

AI assists with:

  C# implementation
  Editor tooling
  Documentation
  Refactoring
  Testing
  Placeholder assets
  Content generation
  Design iteration

Creative ownership remains with the developer.

Every AI-generated contribution is reviewed, refined, and integrated
intentionally.


Success Metrics
---------------
Dark Matter: Genesis succeeds if players consistently describe it as:

  Immersive.
  Atmospheric.
  Intelligent.
  Rewarding.
  Memorable.
  Highly replayable.

Not "large."

Not "feature rich."

Those qualities emerge naturally from strong design.

Chapter 2 - Player Experience
================================================================================

Player Fantasy
--------------
**Kade** is not a hero sent to save the world.

**Kade** is humanity's first commander on Io — founder of a base camp and
leader of the companions who survive there.

Every system should reinforce that identity.

Players are responsible for:

  protecting lives,
  making scientific discoveries,
  building infrastructure,
  expanding civilization,
  deciding humanity's future.


Emotional Progression
---------------------
Phase One
  "I don't think we'll survive."

Phase Two
  "We might actually do this."

Phase Three
  "These people depend on me."

Phase Four
  "We've built something incredible."

Phase Five
  "What legacy will I leave?"

Every chapter of gameplay should move the player naturally through these
emotional states.


Core Gameplay Loop
------------------
Prepare -> Plan -> Explore -> Discover -> Recover -> Return -> Analyze -> Build ->
Expand -> Repeat


Design Rule
-----------
The player should never feel forced into one activity.

Exploration should naturally inspire research.

Research should inspire construction.

Construction should inspire larger expeditions.

The systems create momentum instead of relying on artificial objectives.

Chapter 3 - The World of Io
================================================================================

Design Goal
-----------
Io is not merely the setting.

Io is the game's primary antagonist, greatest mystery, and most important
character.

The player should gradually develop a relationship with the moon itself.

At first it feels hostile.

Later it becomes understandable.

Eventually it becomes almost alive.


Environmental Identity
----------------------
Io should communicate constant instability.

Nothing feels permanent.

Mountains collapse.

New lava rivers emerge.

Sulfur storms, ion lightning, ash gales, and lava surges reshape traversal.

Ancient structures awaken.

Every expedition should feel different because the world itself evolves.


The Living Planet Philosophy
----------------------------
Rather than using scripted events, Io is governed by interconnected systems.

  Geological activity.
  Weather.
  Resonance.
  Wildlife.
  Ancient technology.

These systems influence one another continuously.


Biome Philosophy
----------------
Every biome must satisfy five requirements:

  Unique visual identity.
  Unique survival challenge.
  Unique resource economy.
  Unique environmental storytelling.
  Unique gameplay opportunities.

A biome should never exist solely because it looks different.


Environmental Storytelling
--------------------------
The player should regularly encounter evidence of previous expeditions.

  Collapsed habitats.
  Destroyed mining rigs.
  Abandoned laboratories.
  Emergency broadcasts.
  Personal belongings.

These moments tell the story without requiring long cutscenes.

Chapter 4 - Core Gameplay Pillars
================================================================================

Pillar One - Explore the Unknown
--------------------------------
Exploration drives every progression system.

Without exploration there is no research.

Without research there is no advancement.


Pillar Two - Build a Living Base Camp
-------------------------------------
The base camp is the heart of the game.

Players should care about the people they rescue and the structures they create.


Pillar Three - Scientific Discovery
-----------------------------------
Knowledge is the primary resource.

The player should constantly ask:

"What happens if I investigate this?"


Pillar Four - Meaningful Leadership
-----------------------------------
Leadership is expressed through decisions rather than dialogue.

  Resource allocation.
  Research priorities.
  Expedition planning.
  Risk management.
  Ethical choices involving Echoes.


Pillar Five - Emergent Stories
------------------------------
The best stories come from systems interacting.

Not scripted sequences.

Players should remember:

"The storm hit while my rescue team was halfway home."

Not:

"I completed Mission 14."


================================================================================
Chapter 5 - The Solo Developer Production Strategy
================================================================================

This chapter is unique. Most GDDs don't include it.

Ours must.

Because it is one of the project's greatest strengths.


Production Mission
------------------
Every feature must provide the highest possible player value for the lowest
possible development cost.

This is not about cutting corners.

It is about maximizing impact.


The 80/20 Rule
--------------
Twenty percent of the work should create eighty percent of the player's
experience.

Examples include:

  Dynamic weather instead of hundreds of scripted events.
  Procedural Echo generation instead of hundreds of handcrafted companions.
  Modular building pieces instead of unique architecture for every structure.
  Layered audio and lighting to create atmosphere instead of expensive
  cinematics.


Reusable System Hierarchy
-------------------------
Whenever possible, build one framework that supports many features.

Examples:

  One interaction system for every terminal.
  One inventory system shared by players, containers, drones, and buildings.
  One status effect system used for weather, combat, hazards, and buffs.
  One quest framework for story missions, side missions, and procedural
  expeditions.


AI-First Development
--------------------
Every major gameplay system should be designed with AI-assisted implementation
in mind.

That means:

  Clear interfaces.
  Small, testable classes.
  Data-driven configuration.
  Strong documentation.
  Editor tools that reduce repetitive work.

The goal is to make future coding sessions with AI agents faster, more
consistent, and easier to review.


The "Fun Per Month" Rule
------------------------
This will be our final filter before committing to any feature.

For every proposed mechanic, we'll ask:

How much player enjoyment does this create for every month of development it
requires?

If a feature takes six months to build but only adds a few minutes of meaningful
gameplay, it doesn't belong in Version 1.0.

That doesn't mean we discard good ideas - it means we build them when the
project can support them.

A2. Survival — O2 norm & biome pressures
----------------------------------------
  ALWAYS ON: Oxygen (tanks, deprivation FX, health drain when empty)

  FOUR ENVIRONMENTAL PRESSURES (always-on zone meters — not the same as weather):
    1. Radiation
    2. Cold / Heat — SINGLE thermal stress meter (cold pole ↔ heat pole)
    3. Volcanos (lava exposure, eruption heat, tremor stress)
    4. Sulfur (corrosive saturation in sulfur fields and during sulfur weather)

  DYNAMIC IO WEATHER (director-scheduled events — see A2b):
    Atmospheric: sulfur storms, ion lightning storms, ash gales (+ embedded dust spouts)
    Geological: lava flow surges, geyser field surges, caldera eruption columns,
                tremor swarms
    Magnetospheric: Jovian radiation pulses (often paired with ion lightning)

  SURFACE THREATS (combat / exploration):
    5. Alien life forms — native Io fauna; escalation by biome and Resonance
    6. AI androids — hostile or corrupted machines left from prior expeditions

  SURFACE ENCOUNTERS (expeditions + full map — locked):
    Expeditions and the full map populate with weighted random surface threats
    drawn from encounter tables (aliens, lifeforms, androids). Threats spawn at
    zone anchors when the player enters expedition regions; many patrol linked
    routes (loop / ping-pong) between waypoints. Combat zone budgets cap active
    humanoids near the player. Categories: Alien | Lifeform | Android.
    Prototype: SurfaceEncounterZone + SurfaceEncounterTable + SurfacePatrolRoute.

  Offsets (design target): shelter, caves, inoculations, portable habitat
  (Architect Engineer), environmental suits (upgradeable trio gear).


A2b. Io Weather Lock (canonical — July 2026)
------------------------------------------
Io weather is NOT only sulfur storms. Weather is a director-driven layer on top of
the four environmental pressures. One reusable status-effect + exposure pipeline
handles weather, combat, hazards, and buffs.

WEATHER VS PRESSURE
  Pressures = what a zone IS (radiation field, cold shelf, sulfur basin).
  Weather   = what the planet DOES right now (storm front, lava surge, tremor burst).
  Weather modifies pressure rates, visibility, traversal, and base operations.
  Multiple pressures may spike during one weather event (e.g. caldera eruption
  spikes thermal + volcano + ash particulate).


LOCKED PRIMARY WEATHER EVENTS (ship set)
  ID  Event                    Category        Scope      Base impact
  --  -----------------------  --------------  ---------  ---------------------------
  01  Sulfur Storm             Atmospheric     Global     FULL PAUSE + shelter
  02  Ion Lightning Storm      Atmospheric     Regional   Comms degrade; module chip
  03  Ash Gale                 Atmospheric     Regional   Efficiency down; no pause
  04  Dust Spout Cluster       Atmospheric     Local      Surface-only (see note)
  05  Lava Flow Surge          Geological      Local      Perimeter threat if adjacent
  06  Geyser Field Surge       Hydrothermal    Regional   Surface + exterior module risk
  07  Caldera Eruption Column  Geological      Regional   Heat/ash dome; injury risk
  08  Tremor Swarm             Geological      Local–Reg  Stagger; cave rockfall; stress
  09  Jovian Radiation Pulse   Magnetospheric  Regional   Radiation pressure spike
  10  Resonance Supercell      Composite       Global     FULL PAUSE (Resonance only)

  Dust Spout note: may spawn as its own local event OR as embedded sub-phenomena
  inside Ash Gale (2–4 wandering mini-tornadoes). Never a global base-pause trigger
  on its own.

  Engineering enum mirror: IoWeatherEventKind (Assets/_Project/Scripts/Survival/Weather/).


EVENT BEHAVIOR SUMMARY
  01 Sulfur Storm
     Yellow-corrosive front; visibility collapse; sulfur saturation rise.
     Base: all production/craft queues PAUSE; companions evacuate to Command Center
     rooms. Trio on surface takes full sulfur exposure.
     Phases: Building → Peak → Dissipating.

  02 Ion Lightning Storm
     Jupiter–Io flux arcs; strikes favor exposed metal, high ground, conductive suits.
     Base: radio static, degraded comms templates; unshielded outdoor modules may take
     light damage over time. NO full queue pause.
     Trio: near-miss stagger; hovercraft/turret metal attracts arcs.

  03 Ash Gale
     High-velocity volcanic ash; aim sway; movement penalty; bronze sky.
     Often spawns embedded Dust Spout Cluster (04). Base: reduced power/harvest
     efficiency; exterior work slows. NO full pause.

  04 Dust Spout Cluster
     Small dirt/ash tornadoes — solo wanderer or 2–4 pack across a zone.
     Brief lift, debris chip damage, throws loose loot. Surface-only.

  05 Lava Flow Surge
     Existing lava channel overtops; new flow fingers cut across roads; bridges and
     camps at risk. Timed path denial. Passive lava rivers remain zone geography;
     this event is the SURGE that moves the hazard.

  06 Geyser Field Surge
     Region-wide rhythmic venting: hiss telegraph → steam/sulfur blast → cooldown.
     Marked vent nodes are lethal if stood on. Strong in geyser-field biomes.

  07 Caldera Eruption Column
     Ash plume, heat dome, and fallout in volcano biomes. Spikes thermal (heat pole)
     and volcano pressure. May rain hot ash on expeditions leaving the caldera rim.

  08 Tremor Swarm
     Seismic bursts: camera shake, stagger, cave rockfalls, building stress ticks.
     Short swarms or sustained medium quakes. Caves and lava-tube networks amplify.

  09 Jovian Radiation Pulse
     Fast radiation front from Jupiter shadow/magnetosphere. Often precedes or
     overlaps Ion Lightning Storm (02). Shelter and rad offsets matter.

  10 Resonance Supercell (Memory Core / Resonance Events only)
     Composite: sulfur + ion lightning + tremor at elevated intensity.
     Second event that may trigger FULL base pause. Used for Resonance aftermath —
     not routine scheduler fodder.


BASE OPERATIONS RULE (locked)
  FULL PAUSE + Command Center shelter: Sulfur Storm (01), Resonance Supercell (10).
  All other weather: may reduce efficiency, injure exposed exterior companions, or
  threaten perimeter structures — but does NOT auto-pause every building queue.
  Building Control Overview tab shows active weather + ops state (Running / Reduced /
  PAUSED).


STACKING & SCHEDULER RULES
  One dominant weather event per region at a time.
  Sub-phenomena allowed (dust spouts inside ash gale; tremor during eruption).
  Global events (01, 10) override regional scheduling for base sim.
  Pressures stack with weather — caldera eruption + sulfur storm = extreme zone.
  ExperienceDirector modulates frequency and intensity; WeatherDirector owns phase
  timing (Building → Peak → Dissipating).
  Resonance Events may force or amplify any event; may spawn Supercell (10).


BIOME WEATHER AFFINITY (design target)
  Sulfur plains      — Sulfur Storm, Ash Gale
  Geyser fields      — Geyser Field Surge, Sulfur Storm
  Lava calderas      — Lava Flow Surge, Caldera Eruption, Tremor Swarm
  Ash flats / ridges — Ash Gale, Dust Spout Cluster
  Polar radiation    — Jovian Radiation Pulse, Ion Lightning Storm
  Cave / lava tubes  — Tremor Swarm (localized)
  Precursor ruins    — Any event + Resonance Supercell bias after cores


PLAYER-FACING COMMS (Kairos / Colony Ops)
  Weather warnings use plain names above (not internal IDs).
  Example: "Ash gale building — dust spouts likely near Ridge 7."
  Crisis banner copy for pause events: "SULFUR STORM — BASE OPERATIONS PAUSED" or
  "RESONANCE SUPERCELL — BASE OPERATIONS PAUSED".

A4. Base 22 & Command Center
----------------------------
  Base companions impervious to routine rad / thermal / volcano / surface O2.
  NOT impervious to: sulfur storms, Resonance Supercells, Command Center building
  damage, Strain, or severe exterior weather (eruption fallout, lightning module
  damage, geyser perimeter strikes).

  Weather crisis flow (Sulfur Storm or Resonance Supercell — full pause):
    1. All base work PAUSES
    2. Evacuate to Command Center rooms
    3. Event ends → healthy companions resume jobs

  Reduced-ops weather (ash gale, radiation pulse, ion lightning, etc.):
    Queues may continue at reduced rate; exterior companions at injury risk;
    Building Control Overview shows REDUCED state.

  Building damage: injures / incapacitates — NEVER kills base companions.
  Heal/restore required before they contribute again.


A5. Neural Echoes & procedural generation
-----------------------------------------
  Rescue and reintegrate — do not hire from a shop menu as primary path.
  Echoes are imprints of failed Io expeditions / prior inhabitants.
  Failure can lose Echo permanently.
  Procedural: name format [Io Prefix] [Core Name] [Designation], class, stats,
  abilities, backstory fragment, material tint + modular attachments.
  Progression is local roster / save only — no marketplace trading of Echoes.


A6. Kairos, Memory Cores & Resonance Events
---------------------------------------------
  Kairos is a SPECIAL prologue intelligence — not a generic mission-control bot and not
  a human companion. He is an idle machine / probe shell containing an embedded
  ancient alien Neural Echo who calls himself Kairos.

  PROLOGUE DISCOVERY
    Kade finds the dormant machine early (prologue). Interacting loads a repair
    quest: recover 3 objects that restore the machine enough to awaken.
    Pre-name UI uses liaison shell / probe / ledger labels only.
    **Prologue end:** Kade learns the machine's true name — **Kairos**.

  AWAKENING
    Once repaired, Kairos comes online slightly hostile / angry. He recounts
    that his group was killed by something sinister on Io; they had collected
    Memory Cores once housed in this machine. Without those cores he cannot
    function well or remember much of Io’s past.

  MEMORY CORES → RESONANCE EVENTS
    Find core → recovery setpiece → attach / slot into Kairos → Resonance Event
    (10–15 min world change) + story fragment + unlocks.
    May spike storms, damage Command Center, injure base companions, spawn Echo
    opportunities. Repeat per core (design target: at least 3 primary cores).

  TRUST LADDER (communications)
    Angry / hostile → wary → advisor (mission tips, status help) → friend who
    radios in unprompted and eventually relays chats with other companions.
    Colony Ops / expedition crew handle radio queries until Kairos is awake
    and advisory-unlocked. Personality-flavored Kairos voice is a later comms
    phase; do not treat him as day-one channel traffic.

  RELATIONSHIP TO ECHOES
    Kairos IS an Echo sealed in a machine — distinct from rescueable surface
    Echoes that join the companion roster. He is the mystery hub for Memory Cores
    and Resonance progression.
